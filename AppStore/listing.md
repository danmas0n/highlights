## Description (4000)

You're on the sideline. Your kid does something great. By the time you get your phone up, it's
over.

Highlights fixes that. Put your phone on a tripod, tap the eye, and watch the game. When
something good happens, tap the screen — the previous 25 seconds, plus a few after, become a clip.
No filming the whole game. No scrubbing through ninety minutes on Sunday night to find the two
moments that mattered. Soccer, tennis, lacrosse, basketball — anything with a sideline.

HOW IT WORKS
• While the eye is open, the camera keeps a rolling buffer of only the recent past
• Tap anywhere on the screen to mark a moment. A flash and a buzz confirm it
• The last 25 seconds plus 8 after the tap are saved as a clip. Tap late and it's still there
• Everything else is discarded as you go, so a whole game fits comfortably on your phone

FRAME LATER, NOT DURING
It records in 4K so you can crop in afterwards with no loss of quality. On the sideline, keep your
player somewhere in frame; at home, drag a box around the action and save. A 2× crop of 4K is
exactly 1080p — nothing is upscaled. When the field is far away, switch to your phone's telephoto
lens right from the capture screen.

EDIT ON THE SPOT
• Trim with drag handles, like Photos
• Frame the crop around the action
• Turn the phone sideways for a full-screen look at whether it was actually any good
• Save to your camera roll and send it to the grandparents (or a college coach)

BUILT FOR A REAL SIDELINE
• A game clock that tracks periods, halves, or sets alongside the time of day
• Halftime? Close the eye to rest the phone, open it again for the restart. Every clip stays put
• Optional screen dimming saves battery while still watching and still accepting taps
• Marks survive the app being closed, so a hot phone doesn't cost you the first half
• A Bluetooth camera clicker works if you'd rather not touch the phone

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
There's no account, no server, and no analytics. Footage never leaves your phone unless you save
a clip to Photos. The sideline is full of other people's conversations, so sound is up to you: one
tap turns the microphone off completely, and any clip can be saved without audio. The source code
is public.

I built this for my son's high school and club soccer games. If you've got a kid who plays anything,
I hope it's useful to you too. It's free. There's a tip jar if it earns you a highlight you'd have
missed — it unlocks nothing.

## Notes for App Review

This is a camera app whose main screen is a live viewfinder, so it can look like it "does
nothing" at first glance. The first launch shows a four-screen tour explaining the flow. To
exercise it:

1. Allow camera access when prompted. Microphone access is optional; without it clips are silent.
2. Point the phone at anything and tap the eye button at the bottom centre. It turns red and reads
   "Watching" — the app is now keeping the recent past.
3. Wait about 20 seconds, then tap anywhere on the screen. A white flash and a "Marked" banner
   confirm the moment was saved.
4. Tap the eye again to stop.
5. Tap the Clips icon (bottom left, with a red count badge). The marked moment is listed. Open it
   to play, trim, and frame it; "Save to Photos" exports it to the camera roll.

The microphone button at the top right mutes audio capture; each clip also has an "Include
sound" switch.

The tip jar (the ⓘ About icon, bottom left) contains three consumable in-app purchases. They unlock nothing —
every feature is available without purchase.

No account is required and the app makes no network connections. It must be tested on a physical
device; the Simulator has no camera and the app says so.
